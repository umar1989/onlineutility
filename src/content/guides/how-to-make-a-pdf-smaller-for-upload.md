Few things are more frustrating than finishing an online form and then being told that your PDF is too large. If you want to know how to make a PDF smaller, the good news is that the cause is almost always the same, and the fix takes only a few minutes.

## Why PDFs get so big

A PDF is a container. Inside it can be text, fonts, vector drawings and pictures. A typed document with only text is usually tiny, often under 100 KB. The trouble starts when pages are pictures.

When you scan a certificate or photograph a document, every page is stored as a large image. A modern phone camera produces pictures of several megapixels, so a single page can take 2 to 4 MB. A five-page scan can easily pass 10 MB even though the pages look simple.

So the size of a scanned PDF depends mostly on two things:

- **Resolution:** how many pixels each page image holds.
- **Compression quality:** how strongly each image is squeezed.

Lower either one and the file shrinks. The art is lowering them just enough to meet the limit while keeping the text readable.

## Four ways to reduce PDF size

1. **Scan at a lower setting.** If you have not scanned yet, choose a resolution around 150 to 200 dpi and black and white or greyscale for text pages. Colour is rarely needed for printed certificates, and this can cut the size by more than half.
2. **Remove pages you do not need.** If the upload asks for one document, do not include blank back pages or cover sheets.
3. **Re-compress the pages.** This is what a compression tool does. It redraws each page at a lower resolution and saves it as a smaller image.
4. **Split the document.** If the form allows several uploads, divide the file instead of squeezing everything into one.

## What you lose when you compress

Honesty matters here. To reach a chosen size reliably, a tool must turn pages into pictures. That has two consequences:

- Text inside the PDF can no longer be selected or searched, because it is now part of an image.
- Very strong compression makes small print fuzzy.

For an upload to an application portal, this is normally fine, because a human or a scanner reads the page as an image anyway. For a document that people must search or copy from, keep the original and use a different method.

## A worked example

Imagine a three-page marksheet scan of 6.2 MB, and a form that accepts a file up to 1 MB. The size per page is about 2 MB, so you need to shrink each page by about six times.

Reducing the image resolution from 300 dpi to about 150 dpi cuts the pixel count to a quarter. Compressing the JPEG a little more brings it down again. The result might be around 900 KB: still readable on screen, and now acceptable to the form.

If the tool reports a result that is just above your limit, you have two choices. Remove a page that is not required, or check whether the form allows a slightly larger file. Do not keep re-compressing the same output again and again, because each pass makes it blurrier. Always start from the original.

## Keep your documents private

Many free "compress PDF" websites upload your file to their servers. For a marksheet, an ID card or a bank statement, that means a copy of private information sits on a stranger's machine. ## Checklist before you upload

- Open the new PDF and zoom in. Can you read names, numbers and dates easily?
- Check every page is present and in the right order.
- Confirm the size in KB or MB is under the limit shown on your form. We do not publish official limits for any exam; use the number in your official notification.
- Keep the original file safe and name the compressed copy clearly.
- Use a simple file name without special characters.

## When it still will not fit

If the size is still too large after compression, look at the original scan. A page full of dark photographs or coloured stamps compresses poorly. Re-scan in greyscale, crop away the dark scanner edges, and try again. In many cases a cleaner scan is smaller than any amount of compression applied to a poor one.

A few minutes of care here saves you from last-hour panic before a deadline. Compress, check, and keep your original safe.
