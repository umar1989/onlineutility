Sometimes a PDF is the wrong format. A portal asks for a JPG, a presentation needs a picture of one page, or you want to send a single page over a messaging app. Knowing how to convert PDF pages to images solves all three, and it takes only a minute once you understand the choices involved.

## When you need an image instead of a PDF

- **An upload form accepts only JPG or PNG.** Some portals reject PDFs for certain fields, such as a signature or a photo.
- **You need one page out of many.** Cutting a single page from a long document is easier as a picture.
- **You want to edit or mark the page.** Photo editors and phone markup tools work with images, not PDFs.
- **You are sharing on a platform that previews images.** A picture shows up straight away in a chat, while a PDF needs to be opened.

## JPG or PNG: which one?

Both are image formats, but they behave differently.

| | JPG | PNG |
|---|---|---|
| File size | Smaller | Larger |
| Best for | Scans, photos, pages with pictures | Text, tables, line drawings |
| Quality | Slight loss, hard to see at good settings | No loss |
| Transparency | No | Yes |

As a rule, use JPG when the file size matters, which is most of the time with forms. Use PNG when the page is mostly sharp black text and you want crisp edges.

## Choosing the resolution

Resolution is measured in dpi, dots per inch. A PDF page is a fixed physical size, so more dpi means more pixels and a bigger file.

- **96 dpi** is about what a screen shows. Good for quick previews and small files.
- **150 dpi** is a sensible default. Text is clearly readable and the file stays modest.
- **300 dpi** is print quality. Use it only if you plan to print the image, because files get large quickly.

A quick way to think about it: an A4 page at 150 dpi is roughly 1240 by 1754 pixels. At 300 dpi it is four times as many pixels.

## Step-by-step

1. Open the [PDF to image converter](/pdf-to-image/) and choose your file.
2. Pick JPG or PNG.
3. Pick a resolution. Start at 150 dpi.
4. If you need only certain pages, type them in the pages box. You can write single pages and ranges, such as 1-3, 6.
5. Press convert and wait. Each page appears with a small preview.
6. Download the pages you want, or take all of them in one ZIP file.

## A worked example

Say you have a 12-page prospectus PDF and your project needs pages 4 and 5 as pictures. Type 4-5 in the pages box, choose JPG and 150 dpi. You will get two pictures, perhaps 300 KB each. They are ready to put in a document or send to a friend.

Now suppose the result looks slightly soft when you zoom in. Convert again at 300 dpi for that page only. The picture will be sharper and perhaps 1 MB in size, which is a fair trade for a page you want to print.

## If the image is too big for a form

Conversion gives you a picture, but a form may still have a size limit. Do not guess. Use the number from your official notification, then bring the picture down with a [photo and signature resizer](/photo-and-signature-resizer/), which can target a size in KB. Starting from a 96 or 150 dpi picture helps, because there is less to squeeze.

## Things to watch out for

- **Text is no longer text.** Once a page is a picture, you cannot select or search the words. Keep the PDF if you need that.
- **Password-protected files.** A locked PDF has to be unlocked in your PDF viewer first.
- **Large pages.** Maps and posters can be enormous. Tools limit their size so your browser does not crash; if a page looks smaller than expected, that is the reason.
- **Privacy.** Many online converters upload your file. A browser-based one keeps it on your device, which matters for personal documents.

## Quick checklist

- The right pages were selected and appear in the right order.
- The format and resolution suit what you will do with the image.
- Names and numbers are clear when you zoom in.
- The size is within the limit in your notification, if there is one.
- You still have the original PDF.

With these few rules you can extract any page from any PDF in a form that suits your task.
