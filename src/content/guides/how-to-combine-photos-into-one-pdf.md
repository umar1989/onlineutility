Many application portals ask for a single PDF that holds several documents: a certificate, a mark sheet, an identity card. But the documents on your phone are separate photos. Knowing how to combine photos into one PDF is a small skill that saves a lot of stress, and you do not need any paid software.

## Why a PDF instead of separate pictures

A PDF keeps the pages in a fixed order, prints at a predictable size and opens the same way on every device. A form reviewer can scroll through one file rather than opening five. Portals also tend to limit the number of uploads, so one combined file is often the only way to submit everything.

## Before you start: take good photos

The quality of your PDF depends on the quality of the pictures. Spend two minutes on this step and you will avoid re-doing the whole thing.

- **Use daylight.** Place the document near a window. Avoid using the flash, which creates a white glare patch.
- **Lay the page flat.** Curved paper creates curved text. Put a heavy book on the corners if needed.
- **Fill the frame.** The paper should take up most of the picture, with only a thin margin around it.
- **Hold the phone straight above.** Tilted photos make the page look like a trapezoid.
- **Check the text.** Zoom in on the picture and confirm every word and number is readable.

## Step-by-step: building the PDF

1. **Collect the pictures in one folder.** Rename them in the order you want, such as 1-front, 2-back, 3-marksheet.
2. **Crop the extra background.** A picture of a certificate on a wooden table wastes space and makes the file larger.
3. **Add them to an image to PDF tool.** With our [image to PDF converter](/image-to-pdf/) you can pick several files at once and add more later.
4. **Set the order.** Use the move buttons so that page one is what the reviewer should see first.
5. **Choose the page size.** A4 is the standard paper size and works for most forms. If you want each page to match its picture exactly, use "match each image".
6. **Choose the quality.** Smaller files upload faster and fit stricter limits. Better quality is useful if small print must stay sharp.
7. **Create, then check.** Open the finished PDF and scroll through every page.

## A worked example

Suppose you need to upload your class 10 and class 12 mark sheets, each front and back, as one PDF. That makes four photos. Each photo from a phone is about 3 MB, so the raw total is around 12 MB.

You crop each photo to remove the background and add the four pictures in the order class 10 front, class 10 back, class 12 front, class 12 back. ## Common problems and fixes

- **Pages look sideways.** Some tools ignore the rotation stored in a phone photo. A good tool reads it and rotates automatically. If a page is still wrong, rotate the original picture and add it again.
- **The PDF is huge.** Phone cameras are very high resolution, far beyond what a form needs. Lowering the resolution is safe for documents that people read on a screen.
- **Text looks fuzzy.** Re-take the photo in better light instead of trying to fix it afterwards.
- **Pictures are cut off.** Pick a page size that fits the picture, and leave a small margin so printers do not clip the edges.

## Keep it private

Documents such as mark sheets and identity cards contain personal details. Many free converters upload your pictures to their servers. A tool that runs in your browser is safer, because the pictures stay on your device from start to finish. ## Final checklist

- Pages are in the right order and the right way up.
- Every word is readable at 100 percent zoom.
- The file size is under the limit shown on your form. We do not list limits for specific exams, so use the number in your official notification.
- The file name is short and simple.
- You have kept the original photos in case you need to start again.

A neat, readable PDF makes a better impression than a stack of crooked photos, and now you can produce one in minutes.
