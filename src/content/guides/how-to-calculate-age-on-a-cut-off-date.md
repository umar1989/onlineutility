An application can be rejected for a reason as small as a few days. Age limits are checked on a specific date, not on the day you apply, so learning how to calculate age on a cut-off date is one of the most useful skills for any applicant. This guide explains the method and the traps.

## Why the cut-off date matters

A notification usually says something like "age as on 1 January of the year of examination". That date is fixed for everyone. Your age today, or on the last day to apply, does not matter. Only your age on that named date is compared with the limits.

Two applicants born a week apart can therefore get different results, one eligible and one not, even though they apply on the same day.

## The method, step by step

To work out age in years, months and days, count from the date of birth forward to the cut-off date.

1. **Count full years.** Subtract the birth year from the cut-off year. If the birthday in the cut-off year has not yet arrived on the cut-off date, subtract one.
2. **Count full months.** From the last birthday, count the months until the cut-off date. Stop before you pass the date.
3. **Count the days.** What remains between the last monthly anniversary and the cut-off date is the number of days.

### A worked example

Date of birth: 15 May 2000. Cut-off date: 1 January 2026.

- Last birthday before the cut-off: 15 May 2025. That is 25 complete years.
- From 15 May 2025 to 15 December 2025 is 7 months.
- From 15 December 2025 to 1 January 2026 is 17 days.

Age on the cut-off date: **25 years, 7 months, 17 days**.

When the day of the month in the cut-off is smaller than in the birth date, borrow a month, and use the length of the month just before the cut-off. Doing that by hand is error-prone, which is why an [age calculator for a cut-off date](/age-calculator-for-cut-off-date/) is worth using.

## Understanding the limits

Age rules have two parts: a minimum and a maximum. They are worded in different ways, and the wording changes the answer.

- **"Not more than 27 years"** is often read as exactly 27 years, 0 months and 0 days at most.
- **"Below 28 years"** or **"within 27 completed years"** allows up to 27 years, 11 months and 30 days.
- **"Minimum 18 years"** means you must have completed 18 years by the cut-off date.

These readings differ by almost a whole year. Read the exact sentence in your notification, and if you are unsure, ask the issuing office. Our calculator lets you pick which reading applies and shows the earliest and latest birth dates that fit.

## Relaxation in the upper age limit

Many notifications allow extra years for certain categories of applicants. We do not list those rules, because they differ by organisation and change over time, and an out-of-date figure could mislead you. Take the number of years from the official notification and add it to the maximum age. Then recheck the result against the cut-off date.

## Quick check using birth dates

Sometimes it is faster to compute the cut-off birth date instead of the age. For a maximum of 27 years on 1 January 2026, an applicant must be born on or after 1 January 1999 (when the limit means exactly 27 years). For a minimum of 18 years, they must be born on or before 1 January 2008. Compare your date of birth with those two dates, and you have your answer.

## Leap years

A person born on 29 February has a real birthday only every fourth year. For age limits, count it as 1 March in non-leap years unless your notification says otherwise. Months should be counted using their real length, so February has 28 or 29 days depending on the year.

## Common mistakes

- **Using today's date** instead of the cut-off date.
- **Using the date you filled the form,** which is irrelevant.
- **Ignoring the wording** of "not more than" versus "below".
- **Mixing up the day and month** when typing dates such as 03/04/2000. Choose dates from a date picker where you can, so the format is unambiguous.
- **Using the date on a school certificate** that differs from your other documents. The form usually asks for the date as it appears on your matriculation certificate; check which document your notification names.

## What to do if you are close to the limit

If your result is within a few days of the limit, do not rely on any online tool alone. Check the notification, then confirm the rule by writing to the organisation. Keep a copy of the reply. A short wait now is better than a rejected application later.

## Checklist

- You have the exact cut-off date from the official notification.
- Your date of birth matches your certificate.
- You know whether the limit is "not more than" or "below".
- You added the right relaxation, if any, taken from the notification.
- You have a printed or saved copy of the calculation for your own records.

Age rules are strict but fair when you understand them. A few minutes of careful counting protects your application.
