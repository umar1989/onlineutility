If you have ever filled in an online application and seen the message "file too large", you already know the problem. Learning how to reduce photo size in KB takes about five minutes, and once you understand two simple ideas you will be able to fix almost any photo or signature upload error yourself.

## Pixels and KB are different things

Most people mix these up, and the mix-up causes most failed uploads.

- **Pixels** describe the dimensions of the picture: how wide and how tall it is. A photo that is 200 pixels wide and 230 pixels tall has the same shape wherever you view it.
- **KB (kilobytes)** describe the file size on disk. This is how much storage the file takes, and it is what an upload form usually checks.

Two photos can have identical pixels and very different file sizes. A sharp, detailed picture takes more space than a smooth one, and the same picture saved at high quality takes more space than at low quality. So when a form asks for a certain size in KB, you have two levers: reduce the pixels, or reduce the quality.

## What a form usually asks for

Forms typically give a maximum file size, a format such as JPEG, and sometimes a width and height. We do not list the rules of any particular exam here, because they differ between organisations and change between years. Always copy the numbers from the official notification for the exam you are applying to. Treat anything you read on a blog, including this one, as a starting point only.

## Step-by-step: shrinking a photo

1. **Read your instructions first.** Write down the maximum KB, the pixel size if one is given, and the accepted format.
2. **Start from a good original.** A photo taken in daylight against a plain background compresses better than a dark, noisy one. Do not use a screenshot of a photo if you can avoid it.
3. **Set the pixel size.** If the form gives a width and height, use exactly those. If it gives none, a few hundred pixels on the longer side is usually plenty for a small ID-style picture.
4. **Choose how to fit.** If your original has different proportions from the target, you must either crop it, stretch it or add margins. Cropping keeps faces looking natural, so it is the safest choice for portraits. Stretching distorts faces and is better avoided.
5. **Set the KB limit.** Lower the JPEG quality until the file is under the limit. Quality between 60 and 85 percent usually looks fine on a small picture.
6. **Check the preview.** Look at the face and the text. If a signature looks blotchy, raise the size limit slightly or photograph the signature again with darker ink.

## A worked example

Suppose your instructions say the signature must be 140 pixels wide, 60 pixels tall and under 20 KB. You sign on white paper and take a phone photo, which comes out at about 3 MB and 4000 pixels wide.

First, resize to 140 × 60. That alone makes the picture tiny, and the file may already be under 20 KB. If it is still too large, the quality is reduced until it fits. A 140 × 60 pixel black-on-white signature is simple, so it usually lands well under the limit with good clarity.

Now suppose the photo needs to be 200 × 230 pixels and under 20 KB. Here the pixel size is fixed and the limit is strict, so quality has to drop more. A busy background will make the file larger, so a plain background makes this much easier.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Renaming the extension.** Changing "photo.png" to "photo.jpg" does not change the format. Use a proper converter.
- **Shrinking an already shrunken file again and again.** Each save at lower quality adds blur. Always go back to the original.
- **Ignoring the minimum size.** Some forms also set a minimum. If your result is far below the minimum, raise the quality or pixel size instead.
- **Uploading private files to unknown sites.** Many free resizer websites upload your picture to a server. Use a tool that works inside your browser so your photo never leaves your device.

## When the target cannot be reached

Sometimes the limit is very small compared with the pixel size. A 600 × 800 picture cannot always be squeezed under 20 KB without looking poor. When that happens, check whether you really need those pixels. If the form allows it, reduce the dimensions; if the dimensions are fixed, try a simpler background or a more evenly lit original.

## Final checklist before you upload

- The width and height match the instructions.
- The size in KB is within the limit shown on the form.
- The file opens normally and the face or signature is clear.
- The file name has no unusual characters.

Following this checklist takes a minute and saves you from a rejected form near the deadline. Keep your original photo safe so you can create a new version whenever a different form asks for different numbers.
