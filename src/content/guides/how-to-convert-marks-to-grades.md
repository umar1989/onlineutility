After a test, you have a pile of raw marks and a report card that needs grades. Knowing how to convert marks to grades quickly and consistently saves hours at the end of a term, and it protects you from arithmetic mistakes that cause awkward conversations with parents.

## Marks, percentages and grades

Three different things are easy to confuse.

- **Marks** are the raw score, such as 36.
- **Percentage** is the score as a share of the maximum, such as 72%. This makes tests with different totals comparable.
- **Grade** is a label for a band of percentages, such as B+ for 70 to 79.99.

The path from marks to grade always has two steps: first the percentage, then the band.

## Step 1: calculate the percentage

Percentage = marks obtained ÷ maximum marks × 100

A student with 36 out of 50 has 36 ÷ 50 × 100 = 72%. Always use the maximum for that particular paper, not the maximum of a different one. If a term total combines papers with different maxima, add all marks obtained and all maxima first, then calculate once.

## Step 2: choose the grade bands

A grade table lists the lowest percentage that earns each grade. For example, in an illustrative scale:

| Minimum % | Grade |
|---|---|
| 90 | A+ |
| 80 | A |
| 70 | B+ |
| 60 | B |
| 50 | C |
| 40 | D |
| 0 | E |

This is only an example. Boards and schools publish their own scales, and some use letters, some use words such as "distinction" or "first class", and some attach grade points. Always apply the scale that your institution requires. ## Step 3: apply the bands the same way every time

Read the table from the top. The first row whose minimum is at or below the percentage gives the grade. A student on exactly 80% gets A in the table above, because 80 meets the minimum for A. A student on 79.9% gets B+.

### A worked example

A class test is out of 40. Four students score 38, 31, 27 and 15.

| Student | Marks | Percentage | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 38 | 95% | A+ |
| 2 | 31 | 77.5% | B+ |
| 3 | 27 | 67.5% | B |
| 4 | 15 | 37.5% | E |

With 4 students this is easy by hand. With 40 it is where mistakes creep in, and a tool that takes pasted rows removes the risk.

## Edge cases to decide in advance

- **Boundary marks.** Will 79.5% count as 80? Decide on your rounding policy before you look at the results, and apply it to everybody.
- **Absent students.** Do not enter zero for an absent student unless that is your policy. Leave the row out, or mark it separately.
- **Grace marks.** If your board allows them, add them to the marks first, then compute the grade, and note it.
- **Different maximum marks.** Convert each paper to a percentage separately only if the weights are equal. Otherwise combine totals.

## Keep the process fair and transparent

Share the grade table with students and parents before the test, so no one feels the boundaries were chosen after seeing the results. If you adjust boundaries for a particularly hard paper, record the reason.

## Check your results

After grading a class, look at the distribution. If nobody got a grade above C on a paper you thought was fair, check for a typing error in the maximum marks. If everyone got A, check the paper and the key. A summary of how many students fell in each grade gives you this view at a glance.

## Record keeping

Keep a copy of the marks, the scale you used and the date. A spreadsheet or a CSV file with name, marks, percentage and grade is enough. Pasting from a converter into a spreadsheet takes seconds.

## Checklist

- Maximum marks are correct for the paper.
- The grade table matches your institution's rules.
- Boundary and rounding rules are decided.
- Absentees and special cases are handled consistently.
- The results are checked and saved.
